Sinhala & Tamil · Speech Forensics

Detecting synthetic speech after it's been through the network, not before.

Cadence classifies Sinhala and Tamil audio as real or synthetic under the same low-bitrate compression that messaging apps apply in practice — and shows, region by region, what it heard.

The gap

Clean-audio benchmarks don't survive contact with a phone call.

Most deepfake detectors are trained and tested on studio-clean recordings. Real audio arrives compressed, re-encoded, and lossy — and detector accuracy is known to drop once it does. Add a low-resource language on top, and the gap between benchmark and deployment gets wider still.

What Cadence adds

Four things most detectors handle in isolation, combined into one pipeline:

detects fake speech
tests robustness under compression
explains its own decisions
checks whether those explanations hold up

System features

Five capabilities, one spectrogram at a time.

01 / Detection

Real vs. synthetic

A CNN / ResNet-style backbone operating on Mel spectrograms classifies each clip as human or synthesised speech.

02 / Provenance

Compression provenance

A second head estimates whether the input is clean, lightly compressed, or heavily compressed — so a verdict can be read in context.

03 / Explainability

Grad-CAM heatmaps

Time-frequency regions that drove the decision are highlighted directly on the spectrogram, for forensic inspection.

04 / Calibration

Confidence you can trust

Reported confidence is evaluated for reliability — a model that's certain and wrong under compression isn't useful in the field.

05 / Stability

Explanation stability

The same clip, clean and compressed, is compared to check whether the highlighted regions stay consistent — or drift.
