Project brief

Compression-aware, explainable deepfake detection for Sinhala & Tamil.

Audio deepfake detection, robustness testing, multilingual evaluation, and explainability are usually studied separately. This project combines all four for two low-resource South Asian languages, under the kind of lossy compression real messaging audio actually goes through.

Problem statement

Most detectors are trained and tested on clean, benchmark-style audio, while real-world speech is compressed, noisy, and language-dependent. Performance is known to drop under codec compression and "in the wild" conditions — and multilingual, explainable detection is still an open problem.

Research gap

Robustness, multilingual evaluation, and explainability each have their own literature — but little work brings all three together for low-resource South Asian languages under realistic compression. Comparing clean versus compressed audio, and checking whether explanations stay stable, is where this project sits.

Objectives

What the system sets out to do

  • 01Classify Sinhala and Tamil speech as real or synthetic.
  • 02Evaluate detection on compressed audio, not just clean recordings.
  • 03Generate spectrogram heatmaps that show which regions influenced each prediction.
  • 04Measure how reliable the model's confidence scores actually are.
  • 05Estimate compression provenance — clean, lightly compressed, or heavily compressed.

Model architecture

One backbone, two heads.

A log-Mel spectrogram feeds a CNN / ResNet-18 backbone adapted for single-channel input. Two task-specific heads share that representation, with an optional calibration layer applied to the output.

BACKBONECNN / ResNet-18, single-channel

Data plan

Sources

Real speech — Sinhala and Tamil recordings from Common Voice or comparable public speech corpora.

Synthetic speech — generated with multilingual TTS / voice synthesis tools.

Compression simulation

Low-bitrate, Opus-style compression is applied and decoded back to .wav, producing clean, lightly compressed, and heavily compressed evaluation sets from the same source audio.

Evaluation plan

How results are judged
